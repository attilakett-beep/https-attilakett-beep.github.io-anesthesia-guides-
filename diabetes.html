Insulin dose guidance: what is deliberately excluded?

The ADA 2026 standards contain general suggestions for some preoperative insulin dose adjustments. This prototype does not calculate or prescribe doses; reductions, pump settings, correction scales, and infusion plans must be governed by patient-specific orders and an institutional protocol. Basal insulin must not be inadvertently omitted in type 1 diabetes.

Step 3 of 5 · GLP-1 / aspiration

GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 medications

Guidelines increasingly favor individualized continuation with aspiration-risk assessment, not a universal one-week medication hold.

2024 multisociety guidance targets higher-risk patients; 2025 SPAQI suggests clear liquids for all GLP-1 users. Do not silently substitute one protocol for the other.

Clinical risk factors (select if present)

Why GLP-1 decisions are individualized

Risk assessment considers GI symptoms, dose escalation, dose and formulation, urgency, coexisting gastric motility conditions, and anesthesia plan.

2024 multisociety approach

Most lower-risk patients may continue GLP-1 therapy. Elevated-risk patients may benefit from a liquid-only diet for at least 24 hours, gastric ultrasound where available, and modified anesthesia planning.

Source: multisociety practice guidance (2024).
2025 SPAQI approach

Recommends continuation without significant GI symptoms, with a clear-liquid fasting approach for 24 hours before procedures requiring anesthesia. This is more prescriptive than the 2024 high-risk–targeted dietary approach.

Source: SPAQI multidisciplinary consensus (2025).

03 · Ketone and acid-base assessment

For the urgent missed-hold pathway, the uploaded source uses blood ketones, BMP / anion gap and pH. Local test availability and clinical interpretation are essential.

The supplied decision map uses ketone bands <1.5, 1.5–3, >3 mmol/L and anion gap >14 or pH <7.3. Exactly 1.5 or 3.0, pH 7.3 and discordant acid-base findings require clinician interpretation. None are automated proceed criteria.

    • Ketones <1.5 mmol/L: lower biochemical concern in source map, not clearance.
    • Ketones 1.5–3 mmol/L: check anion gap and pH; exact boundaries and discordance need clinical review.
    • Ketones >3 mmol/L, anion gap >14 mEq/L, pH <7.3, or low bicarbonate: evaluate for ketoacidosis.
    • Symptoms can override reassuring lab results; glucose alone is insufficient to exclude euglycemic DKA.
    Implementation approval checklist and QI metrics

    For institution-wide deployment, anesthesia/endocrinology/pharmacy must approve:

    • Elective exception and rescheduling rules; procedure and fasting considerations.
    • Exact values of 1.5 and 3.0 mmol/L; discordant anion gap and pH, and the “and/or” source wording.
    • Point-of-care versus serum ketone testing and facility capability.
    • Management of emergency cases, monitoring frequency, disposition and restart criteria.

    Potential QI measures: on-time drug holds, missed-hold rescheduling, urgent-case metabolic assessment, postoperative eDKA, unplanned admissions, and follow-up.

    References & governance

    Independent summaries of public professional guidance. The exact clinical rules and patient instructions require local approval.

    1. Uploaded source: SGLT2 Inhibitors — Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis presentation, especially slides 6, 9–13. Detailed drug timing, missed-hold branches, and thresholds are attributed to this source; this implementation is independently designed and clinically unvalidated.
    2. ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026, Section 16. Perioperative glycemic target, insulin, oral drugs, SGLT2 inhibitors, individualized GLP-1 management.
    3. Multisociety GLP-1 clinical practice guidance (2024). Patient-specific continuation, risk factors, liquid diet when elevated risk, aspiration mitigation.
    4. SPAQI multidisciplinary GLP-1 consensus (2025). Continuation without significant GI symptoms, differing fasting approach, reinstitution.
    5. 2026 AHA/ACC perioperative cardiovascular guideline reaffirmation. Additional considerations for diabetes therapy, including an approach to metformin that differs from ADA guidance.
    Institutional policy decisions needed

    GLP-1 liquid diet for high-risk patients versus all patients; GLP-1 hold/restart process; metformin policy; insulin and pump orders; day-of-surgery glucose monitoring and treatment; SGLT2 missed-hold testing and urgent-case escalation; implementation in ambulatory sites.

    Clinical rules are abbreviated and will not cover all product-label, contraindication, pregnancy, pediatric, inpatient, or disease-specific exceptions. No treatment can be authorized based on this prototype alone.
