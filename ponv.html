Step 2 · Reduce baseline risk

  • Use opioid-sparing multimodal analgesia and regional techniques where appropriate.
  • Consider propofol-based total intravenous anesthesia when appropriate; minimize volatile agents and nitrous oxide exposure.
  • Maintain appropriate hydration and tailor neuromuscular blockade reversal to the clinical context.

These are protocol-derived considerations, not automated directives. The anesthesia team must determine applicability.

Before choosing antiemetics

  • Review previously administered agents and duplicate classes.
  • Check QTc/TdP risk, interacting medications and electrolyte abnormalities.
  • Assess patient-specific restrictions, including age, diabetes, allergies and formulary availability.

After rescue treatment, have symptoms resolved?

Core rescue rules

  • Review which prophylactic and intraoperative antiemetic classes were used.
  • For breakthrough PONV, select a different pharmacologic class from a previously administered class.
  • Do not repeat a medication class until at least 6 hours have elapsed under the source protocol; consult current local policy.
  • If symptoms persist after additional treatment, notify anesthesiology and reassess other causes.

Validation checklist for institutional adoption

Clinical decisions

  • Approve the intended population and exclusions (adult, non-pregnant).
  • Decide whether to retain the eight-item source-specific risk screen or adopt a validated scoring tool.
  • Approve prevention tiers and choices of prophylactic drug classes.
  • Approve PACU rescue escalation and the 6-hour re-dosing rule.
  • Define QTc/electrolyte, age and diabetes precautions.

Operational decisions

  • Confirm formulary availability and local dosing/monitoring requirements.
  • Determine who documents risk assessment and medication administration.
  • Test cases with prior antiemetic exposure, QT risks, and persistent symptoms.
  • Set policy version, approver, review date and a reporting mechanism.
  • Do not connect to patient records or orders without privacy and IT review.
Suggested first pilot: use hypothetical cases to verify the dynamic pathway against a pharmacist- and anesthesiologist-approved PONV protocol. Do not use this prototype as a prescribing tool.
